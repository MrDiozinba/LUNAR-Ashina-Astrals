LUNAR — Lunar Environment Intelligence and Mission Planning System

LUNAR is a prototype decision-support application for understanding terrain-obstructed Sun and geometric Earth visibility at a provisional lunar South Pole surface location. It combines NASA Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA) topography at multiple spatial resolutions with DE440-based SPICE ephemerides to build azimuth-dependent terrain horizons and calculate apparent-disk visibility over time. Its Streamlit dashboard presents terrain skylines, celestial geometry, visibility timelines, observer-height sensitivity and sampled window comparisons. The team independently compared four 577-epoch SPICE outputs (Sun and Earth at 1 m and 5 m observer heights) with JPL Horizons; differences were at or below 0.000024 degrees in elevation. The current demonstration is limited to one provisional Site01 tile-centre test point, a 45 km terrain range and a 48-hour timeline; it does not model DSN link availability or electrical power.
